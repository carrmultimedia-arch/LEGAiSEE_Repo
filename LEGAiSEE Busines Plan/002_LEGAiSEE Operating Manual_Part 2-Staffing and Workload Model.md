# LEGAiSEE

## Operating Manual — Part 2: Staffing and Workload Model

**Document status:** Initial operating design **Business stage:** Pre-revenue / pre-launch **Human workforce:** Founder only **Target operating model:** Founder-led business supported by a small number of specialized AI capabilities **Primary constraint:** Generate revenue while developing the minimum infrastructure needed to deliver the work reliably.

---

# 1. The Fundamental Staffing Decision

LEGAiSEE does not need 35 employees, 35 AI agents, or even 12 separate AI agents to begin operating.

It needs a small number of clearly defined responsibilities, a dependable process for moving work between them, and an accountable person who can approve consequential decisions.

The 12 pipelines in Part 1 describe the work LEGAiSEE must eventually perform. They do not represent 12 full-time jobs.

At launch, the same person or AI capability can cover multiple functions.

For example, prospect research, historical research, source collection, and preliminary evidence processing share many of the same underlying activities. They do not necessarily require four different agents.

Likewise, an AI Producer and an AI Director do not initially require two separate models. They require two distinct operating responsibilities that can be performed by one model using different instructions and task contexts.

**The recommended starting structure is one human principal supported by four logical AI roles.**

These are organizational roles, not a claim that the current Command Center already supports four functioning agents.

## Recommended launch structure

| Position | Level | Initial staffing | Primary responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founder / Principal | Human authority | 1 person | Business ownership, sales, client relationships, commercial decisions, final approvals |
| Producer | L4 | AI-assisted role | Converts business objectives into project plans and measurable outcomes |
| Director | L3 | AI-assisted role | Coordinates tasks, dependencies, assignments, deadlines, and exceptions |
| Research &amp; Evidence Operator | L2 | AI-assisted role | Prospect research, source discovery, artifact processing, evidence verification |
| Analysis &amp; Deliverables Operator | L2 | AI-assisted role | Analytical synthesis, authority reconstruction, report preparation, quality checks |
| Operations Assistant | L1 | AI-assisted role or task mode | Organization, recordkeeping, formatting, reminders, routine administrative support |

The four AI roles are functional assignments. They could initially run through one general-purpose LLM, with different prompts and structured inputs.

There is no requirement to purchase four AI subscriptions or run four independent agents.

## What this structure accomplishes

The Producer decides what needs to happen.

The Director determines how the work should be coordinated.

The Operators execute defined assignments.

The Assistant handles supporting tasks.

The founder remains the final authority for business commitments, pricing, client-facing conclusions, and other consequential decisions.

This mirrors the production-company hierarchy without confusing organizational responsibility with technical permissions.

An agent assigned the Producer role does not automatically receive permission to change the production server, spend money, send client communications, or approve its own work.

Those permissions must be granted separately.

# 2. Founder / Principal

**Organizational level:** Human executive authority **Initial headcount:** One **Primary objective:** Establish a viable business, acquire paying clients, and ensure the work meets LEGAiSEE's standards.

The founder is not simply another worker in the system. The founder is responsible for the business itself.

The AI workforce should reduce the amount of time the founder spends on research, preparation, organization, and routine production so that more time can be devoted to revenue generation, judgment, and client relationships.

## Primary responsibilities

### Business development

* Define the initial market.
* Identify the most commercially promising prospects.
* Establish the first offer to sell.
* Conduct important prospect conversations.
* Approve proposals and commercial commitments.
* Maintain relationships with potential referral sources.

### Methodology and quality

* Define what constitutes a defensible finding.
* Approve the research methodology.
* Resolve important interpretive questions.
* Review material conclusions.
* Approve final client deliverables.

### Financial control

* Approve spending.
* Monitor cash flow.
* Establish pricing and payment terms.
* Review financial obligations.
* Decide when additional tools or services are justified.

### System oversight

* Approve consequential changes to the production environment.
* Review security and backup arrangements.
* Decide which capabilities should be automated.
* Establish agent permissions.
* Review operational exceptions.

## Daily operating schedule

The following is a suggested allocation for a normal five-day working week. It is not a requirement to work these exact hours.

| Activity                  | Suggested daily allocation | Purpose                                                          |
|---|---------------------------:|---|
|Revenue-generating activity|2 hours                     |Prospecting review, outreach, conversations, proposals, referrals |
|Product and methodology    |1.5 hours                   |Refine the offer, validate the process, improve delivery materials|
|Command Center / operations|1 hour                      |Resolve blockers, test workflows, review system status            |
|Research and client work   |1.5 hours                   |Review evidence, conduct important analysis, prepare deliverables |
|Planning and administration|30 minutes                  |Review priorities, finances, deadlines, and exceptions            |
|**Total**                  |**6.5 hours**               |Suggested working day                                             |

This allocation is a starting hypothesis, not a rigid schedule.

Before the first client, revenue-generating activity should receive the largest share of available time. Once a paid engagement begins, delivery requirements will temporarily take precedence.

The critical point is that system development must not consume every available working hour.

A sophisticated internal operating system is not a substitute for a paying customer.

## What the founder should stop doing manually

As reliable tools become available, the founder should progressively stop spending time on:

* Reformatting research into standard records.
* Copying the same information between systems.
* Manually checking every basic field in a prospect record.
* Repeatedly writing the same administrative instructions.
* Searching for files that should have a defined location.
* Reconstructing the status of a project from scattered notes.
* Rewriting routine reports from scratch.
* Performing repetitive checks that can be reliably automated.

The founder should continue performing tasks that require commercial judgment, personal trust, specialized interpretation, or consequential approval.

# 3. L4 Producer — Business and Project Decision Maker

**Role:** Producer / Decision Maker **Organizational level:** L4 **Initial implementation:** A defined AI operating mode, not necessarily a separate agent.

The Producer receives an objective and determines what must be accomplished to achieve it.

For example:

> Identify 25 established businesses along the San Antonio–Austin corridor that may have lost historical authority signals, evaluate the evidence supporting each opportunity, and produce a prioritized prospect list for founder review.

The Producer should convert that objective into an actionable project.

## Responsibilities

1. Interpret the approved objective.
2. Define the desired outcome.
3. Identify the workstreams required.
4. Establish acceptance criteria.
5. Estimate the resources and time required.
6. Identify dependencies and risks.
7. Propose the task list.
8. Assign tasks through the Director.
9. Evaluate the completed project against its original objective.
10. Recommend the next action.

## Daily tasks

The Producer does not need to generate new work continuously.

It should activate when a meaningful event occurs.

Examples include:

* The founder defines a new business objective.
* A new prospect meets a qualification threshold.
* A client engagement is approved.
* A research project reaches a decision point.
* A deliverable fails quality review.
* An unresolved issue requires a change in the plan.
* The Director reports that a project is blocked.

For a small operation, the Producer can prepare a daily priority list and an end-of-day status report.

## Required outputs

Every Producer assignment should result in a documented plan containing:

* Objective.
* Desired business outcome.
* Project scope.
* Required workstreams.
* Task list.
* Task dependencies.
* Required resources.
* Acceptance criteria.
* Risks and assumptions.
* Approval requirements.
* Completion conditions.

## Decision boundaries

The Producer may recommend a change in approach, but it cannot independently:

* Change a client's contracted scope.
* Commit LEGAiSEE to a new price.
* Approve an unbudgeted expense.
* Make a binding promise to a prospect.
* Authorize unrestricted external communications.
* Override the founder's confidentiality or security requirements.

## Initial workload

**Estimated demand:** Approximately 2–5 hours per week of equivalent planning and review activity during the pre-launch period.

This does not mean the founder must spend that much time interacting with a Producer agent. It represents the work that the role may perform.

The Producer should not operate as a continuously running autonomous process. It should work when an objective, milestone, or exception warrants its involvement.

# 4. L3 Director — Operations and Coordination Manager

**Role:** Manager / Director **Organizational level:** L3 **Initial implementation:** A task-coordination workflow with explicit status tracking.

The Director converts the Producer's project plan into coordinated execution.

The Producer answers:

**What needs to be accomplished, and why?**

The Director answers:

**Who or what will do each task, in what order, and how will completion be verified?**

## Responsibilities

* Assign tasks to appropriate Operators.
* Confirm that required inputs are available.
* Track dependencies.
* Monitor task status.
* Identify overdue work.
* Review outputs against task requirements.
* Request corrections.
* Escalate unresolved problems.
* Maintain project status.
* Report completion and remaining risks.

## Daily tasks

The Director's daily operating cycle should be event-driven.

### Start of day

1. Review open projects and tasks.
2. Identify overdue or blocked work.
3. Confirm the next actions.
4. Prioritize tasks based on deadlines and dependencies.

### During execution

1. Receive Operator outputs.
2. Check whether required fields and evidence are present.
3. Route incomplete work back for correction.
4. Escalate contradictions or permission issues.
5. Update project status.

### End of day

1. Summarize completed tasks.
2. Identify outstanding work.
3. Record blockers.
4. Prepare the next operational priorities.
5. Notify the founder of decisions requiring approval.

## Required outputs

* Task assignments.
* Project status.
* Dependency status.
* Exception reports.
* Quality-control results.
* Escalation requests.
* Completion reports.

## What the Director must not do

The Director must not equate a completed task status with a verified result.

For example, an Operator might report that it has identified three historical sources for a claim. The Director must still determine whether the sources are available, relevant, and sufficiently independent for the intended use.

The Director should also avoid repeatedly asking agents to perform the same work when a task is blocked by a missing input or a system limitation.

A blocked task needs a diagnosis, not another identical attempt.

## Initial workload

**Estimated demand:** Approximately 3–7 hours per week of equivalent coordination activity during pre-launch operations.

The actual demand will depend on the number of concurrent projects and the quality of the task-tracking system.

Initially, the founder can review a single consolidated task board rather than monitoring multiple agents individually.

# 5. L2 Research &amp; Evidence Operator

**Role:** Research specialist **Organizational level:** L2 **Initial implementation:** Highest-priority specialist capability.

This role supports both the revenue pipeline and the eventual client-delivery pipeline.

That makes it the most immediately useful Operator to develop.

## Primary assignments

### Prospect research

* Identify established businesses.
* Locate official websites and public business records.
* Research company history.
* Identify relevant historical events and changes.
* Locate potential decision-makers through appropriate public sources.
* Document source links and research dates.
* Identify possible authority-reconstruction opportunities.

### Evidence acquisition

* Inventory client-authorized files.
* Extract available metadata.
* Organize artifacts.
* Identify relevant entities, dates, events, and claims.
* Record provenance.
* Flag missing information.

### Verification

* Find corroborating sources.
* Identify contradictory evidence.
* Assess whether sources are independent.
* Distinguish documented facts from interpretations.
* Identify gaps requiring additional research.
* Prepare unresolved questions for human review.

## Daily task examples

A normal assignment might be:

1. Receive a prospect or research question.
2. Read the assignment and acceptance criteria.
3. Search approved sources.
4. Collect relevant findings.
5. Record source references.
6. Compare findings across sources.
7. Identify uncertainty.
8. Produce a structured report.
9. Flag claims that cannot be established.
10. Submit the report to the Director.

## Required output format

Every substantial research assignment should produce a consistent record.

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Research ID | Unique identifier for the assignment |
| Research question | What needs to be established |
| Finding | The proposed answer or observation |
| Source | Where the information was obtained |
| Source date | Publication or record date, when available |
| Access date | When the source was examined |
| Source type | Official record, company publication, newspaper, archive, interview, etc. |
| Independence | Whether the source independently supports the finding |
| Contradictions | Relevant conflicting information |
| Confidence | Assessment based on the evidence |
| Limitations | What remains unknown |
| Status | Unreviewed, verified, disputed, or unresolved |

The specific status vocabulary should be standardized before it is used throughout the Command Center.

## Workload estimates

During pre-launch prospecting:

* Initial prospect research: approximately 15–45 minutes per business, depending on complexity.
* More detailed historical research: approximately 1–3 hours per business.
* Verification of a specific claim: approximately 15–60 minutes, depending on source availability.

These are planning estimates, not established LEGAiSEE performance benchmarks. Actual times should be measured during the first 10–20 assignments.

AI can accelerate discovery and organization, but web access, source quality, paywalls, inaccessible archives, and verification requirements may still require substantial human effort.

## Quality rule

The Operator must never fabricate a source, imply that an inaccessible page was reviewed, or describe a claim as verified merely because several sources repeat it.

A research output with unresolved findings can still be useful. An apparently complete report containing invented certainty is not acceptable.

# 6. L2 Analysis &amp; Deliverables Operator

**Role:** Analytical specialist and report producer **Organizational level:** L2 **Initial implementation:** Begin with bounded analytical assignments and document production.

This role becomes more important once LEGAiSEE has paid engagements.

Before that point, it should help refine sample deliverables and test the methodology using appropriately selected public information.

## Primary assignments

* Synthesize verified findings.
* Compare historical and current positioning.
* Identify documented changes in business identity or market presence.
* Examine historical authority signals.
* Identify inconsistencies between available evidence and current public presentation.
* Prepare evidence-linked analytical summaries.
* Assemble dossiers and reports.
* Produce tables, timelines, and relationship summaries.
* Check deliverables against the approved scope.
* Identify unsupported conclusions.

## Daily task examples

1. Receive a defined analytical question.
2. Review the approved source findings.
3. Identify relevant patterns.
4. Separate observations from interpretations.
5. Connect conclusions to evidence.
6. Prepare the requested analytical output.
7. Run a completeness and consistency check.
8. Submit the result for review.

## Required outputs

Depending on the assignment, outputs may include:

* Historical timeline.
* Business identity record.
* Authority-signal inventory.
* Evidence-linked findings.
* Historical positioning analysis.
* Competitive distinction analysis.
* Voice-profile analysis.
* Gap analysis.
* Proposed reconstruction.
* Client-facing report or dossier.

Not every commission requires every output. The contracted scope determines what must be produced.

## Workload estimates

For planning purposes:

* Preliminary synthesis: approximately 1–3 hours.
* A focused analytical report: approximately 3–8 hours.
* A complex, evidence-heavy deliverable: potentially 10–30 hours or more.

These are provisional estimates. The first paid commissions should be used to measure actual production effort and determine whether published pricing supports the required work.

## Quality rule

The Operator must distinguish three different things:

1. **Historical evidence:** What the records establish.
2. **Analytical interpretation:** What the evidence suggests.
3. **Present-day recommendation:** What LEGAiSEE proposes doing with that understanding.

Combining these categories without clearly distinguishing them can make a plausible interpretation look like a historical fact.

# 7. L1 Operations Assistant

**Role:** Production assistant / administrative support **Organizational level:** L1 **Initial implementation:** A task mode within the specialist roles, not a separate agent.

The Assistant handles supporting tasks that are repetitive, structured, and relatively low-risk.

It should reduce friction between the more demanding stages of work.

## Responsibilities

* Organize files.
* Standardize filenames.
* Prepare folders and project templates.
* Extract basic document metadata.
* Check required fields.
* Format approved material.
* Prepare meeting notes from authorized source material.
* Compile status summaries.
* Identify missing documents.
* Prepare reminders.
* Update routine records.
* Check links and document completeness.
* Assemble administrative packets.

## Tasks to avoid delegating without review

The Assistant should not independently:

* Delete original evidence.
* Alter source documents.
* Make factual corrections to historical records without a documented review.
* Send binding commercial communications.
* Modify access permissions.
* Make financial commitments.
* Publish client information.
* Change production-server files.

## Initial workload

**Estimated demand:** Approximately 2–5 hours per week of equivalent administrative work, depending on how much recordkeeping is automated.

This role is best treated as a collection of reusable task procedures.

Creating a separate agent for every clerical activity would add complexity without a demonstrated benefit.

|Director coordination        |3–7 hours                   |4–8 hours                     |

|**Total equivalent workload**|**19–40 hours**             |**29–68 hours**               |

A high price does not make an unbounded project profitable.

# 9. Founder-Only Operating Model

The first version of LEGAiSEE should not attempt to automate every pipeline.

It should support the shortest dependable path from prospect to payment, followed by a controlled delivery process.

## Stage 1 — Before the first paying client

### Daily priorities

**Priority 1: Revenue**

* Identify a manageable number of promising prospects.
* Research their business history and likely needs.
* Prepare relevant, evidence-based outreach.
* Follow up on prior conversations.
* Seek referrals and introductory meetings.

**Priority 2: Minimum viable delivery**

* Define one service that can be explained clearly.
* Establish its scope and deliverables.
* Create a repeatable research procedure.
* Prepare a sample deliverable using public information.
* Estimate the actual hours required to complete it.

**Priority 3: System readiness**

* Verify that the Command Center is appropriately secured.
* Confirm where prospect records are stored.
* Establish a reliable task list.
* Ensure that original evidence and derived findings can be distinguished.
* Reconcile the commercial and payment configuration before accepting money.

**Priority 4: Expansion**

* Add automation only when a recurring problem has been identified.
* Defer advanced dashboards, autonomous agents, and complex orchestration.
* Avoid building capabilities that do not support sales, delivery, security, or immediate operational reliability.

## Stage 2 — First paid engagement

Once a client commits, the operating priorities change.

1. Confirm the agreement and payment requirements.
2. Create the engagement record.
3. Establish the scope and acceptance criteria.
4. Collect authorized client inputs.
5. Execute the research and verification process.
6. Review the analysis and deliverables.
7. Deliver the contracted work.
8. Record actual hours, costs, problems, and client feedback.
9. Use the results to improve the next engagement.

The first client is also a process-validation exercise. The goal is not to experiment carelessly with the client's records; it is to determine which internal procedures work, which require revision, and which tasks consume more time than anticipated.

## Stage 3 — Repeatable delivery

After several comparable engagements, LEGAiSEE should have enough operational evidence to determine:

* Which tasks recur.
* Which tasks can be automated reliably.
* Which steps require human review.
* Which service elements consume the most time.
* Which deliverables clients value most.
* Whether current pricing supports the workload.
* Whether a contractor, employee, or additional AI capability would improve capacity.

Staffing should follow demonstrated demand rather than the theoretical completeness of the organizational chart.

# 10. What Should Be Built First in the Command Center?

The proposed organization can exist conceptually before every supporting software feature is available.

The first implementation should focus on the minimum records and workflows needed to coordinate work reliably.

## Minimum requirement 1: Task management

Each task needs:

* A unique ID.
* A clear objective.
* An assigned role.
* A status.
* Required inputs.
* Expected output.
* Acceptance criteria.
* A due date or scheduling rule.
* Dependencies, where applicable.
* A record of completion or failure.

A task should not disappear merely because an agent returns an answer.

## Minimum requirement 2: Project management

Each project needs:

* A project objective.
* An accountable owner.
* A task list.
* Milestones.
* Current status.
* Outstanding decisions.
* Known risks.
* A completion standard.

The project record should make it possible to answer a simple question: What needs to happen next?

## Minimum requirement 3: Evidence records

Each research finding must be connected to its source.

At a minimum, the system needs to distinguish:

* Original artifact.
* Extracted information.
* Proposed claim.
* Verified finding.
* Analytical interpretation.
* Final approved conclusion.

These distinctions are central to LEGAiSEE's credibility.

## Minimum requirement 4: Approval controls

The system should record when a consequential action requires founder approval.

Examples include:

* Sending a proposal.
* Changing commercial terms.
* Accepting a material scope change.
* Publishing a significant factual claim.
* Delivering final client work.
* Changing production-server code.
* Authorizing an expenditure.

## Minimum requirement 5: Audit trail

For material work, the system should record what happened, when it happened, and what evidence supports the resulting status.

An audit trail is especially important when AI performs multiple steps or when a finding changes during review.

### Implementation caution

The presence of a Command Center module does not establish that the underlying workflow works end to end.

Before depending on any module, test its actual behavior: input, database write, status change, permissions, error handling, and resulting output.

The existing system should be inspected and tested before new modules are added.

# 11. Agent Implementation Sequence

The four-level hierarchy should be introduced in a controlled sequence.

## First: L2 Research Operator

Start with a bounded research assignment that has a clear expected output.

For example:

> Research one established business using approved public sources. Produce a dated company profile, historical timeline, source inventory, and list of unresolved claims. Do not invent missing information.

This provides a practical test of research quality and structured output.

## Second: L1 Assistant capabilities

Add reusable tasks for:

* Formatting research records.
* Checking required fields.
* Organizing files.
* Producing status summaries.
* Preparing draft reports from approved inputs.

These tasks should use the outputs already produced by the Research Operator.

## Third: L3 Director

Once individual tasks work reliably, introduce coordination.

The Director should assign work, monitor status, validate completion requirements, and escalate exceptions.

At this stage, a structured task board and a capable LLM may be sufficient. A fully autonomous multi-agent framework is not a prerequisite.

## Fourth: L4 Producer

Introduce project-level planning after the task and coordination layers are dependable.

The Producer should generate plans using real operational knowledge: actual task types, actual constraints, actual acceptance criteria, and observed completion times.

This avoids building a sophisticated planning layer around imaginary capabilities.

## Fifth: Bounded orchestration

Only after the earlier stages work should LEGAiSEE consider automatically routing work between agents or executing multi-step workflows without direct founder intervention.

The orchestration layer should be introduced only when it solves a demonstrated coordination problem.

# 12. Capacity and Hiring Triggers

Do not hire based solely on the amount of work the business could theoretically perform.

Use observable operating conditions.

| Trigger | Appropriate response |
| --- | --- |
| Prospect research is taking too much founder time | Improve the research procedure and automate routine collection |
| Follow-ups are being missed | Improve task scheduling and CRM reminders |
| Evidence is frequently incomplete | Strengthen source and artifact intake procedures |
| Deliverables require repeated corrections | Improve acceptance criteria and quality control |
| A paid engagement exceeds available capacity | Reduce scope, extend the timeline, or obtain qualified assistance |
| Several engagements have similar repetitive workloads | Evaluate a reusable workflow or specialized operator |
| Client work consistently displaces sales activity | Consider additional delivery capacity |
| Workload is recurring and revenue supports the cost | Evaluate a contractor, employee, or paid automation |
| A technical task requires expertise the founder lacks | Consider a narrowly scoped specialist engagement |

## Recommended staffing order

When revenue supports expansion, the likely sequence is:

1. **Specialist contractor:** For a specific skill gap or a defined workload that cannot be completed reliably in-house.
2. **Research or production support:** If repeatable delivery work consistently consumes the founder's time.
3. **Operations support:** If coordination and administration become a persistent bottleneck.
4. **Additional specialized AI capabilities:** When distinct tasks benefit from dedicated instructions, tools, permissions, or evaluation procedures.
5. **Broader staffing:** Only when the volume and economics justify it.

The sequence may change based on actual client demand.

The objective is not to minimize human staffing at any cost. It is to build a profitable operating model in which humans and AI each perform the work they are best suited to perform.

# 13. Operating Rules for the Initial AI Workforce

The following rules apply to every AI role.

### Rule 1: One accountable owner

Every task has one responsible owner, even when several agents contribute.

### Rule 2: Defined inputs and outputs

Agents receive a specific assignment and return an output that can be evaluated.

### Rule 3: No invented completion

An agent must not claim to have accessed a website, read a file, changed a database, sent a message, or completed an action unless that action actually occurred.

### Rule 4: Preserve provenance

Research findings must remain connected to their sources. Derived summaries must not replace original evidence.

### Rule 5: Escalate material uncertainty

If a task cannot be completed reliably, the agent should identify the blocker and request a decision rather than manufacture an answer.

### Rule 6: Separate recommendations from authorization

An agent may recommend an action without having permission to execute it.

### Rule 7: Use the least necessary permission

Agents should have only the access required for their assignments.

### Rule 8: Record consequential actions

Important decisions, external communications, production changes, and client-facing approvals must be traceable.

### Rule 9: Measure actual performance

Track quality, completion time, failure rate, and human correction effort. Do not assume that a workflow is successful merely because it produces output quickly.

### Rule 10: Keep the founder in control

The system exists to extend the founder's capacity, not to create an unaccountable organization operating beyond the founder's understanding or authorization.

# 14. Staffing Decision

The recommended initial configuration is:

**One founder + one LLM capability operating in four defined roles + the existing Command Center where its functionality has been verified.**

The four roles are:

* L4 Producer.
* L3 Director.
* L2 Research &amp; Evidence Operator.
* L2 Analysis &amp; Deliverables Operator.

L1 assistance begins as reusable task procedures within those roles.

No additional human employee is required merely to establish the operating structure.

No dedicated subscription is required for every role.

No autonomous multi-agent framework is required before LEGAiSEE has demonstrated that the underlying tasks can be performed reliably.

The initial system should help the founder do three things well:

1. Find appropriate prospects.
2. Deliver a clearly scoped, defensible service.
3. Learn from each engagement so the next one becomes more efficient.

**The next part of the operating manual should define the staff avatars and role instructions in operational detail: each role's mission, authority, required knowledge, daily task procedures, handoff requirements, performance measures, and training plan.**
