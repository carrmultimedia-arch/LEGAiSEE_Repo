# LEGAiSEE

## Operating Manual — Part 1: Business Pipeline Architecture

**Document status:** Initial operating design **Business stage:** Pre-launch **Human workforce:** Founder only **Automation objective:** Maximum practical automation without compromising research quality, confidentiality, or client trust.

---

# 1. Operating Principles

LEGAiSEE's operating model must satisfy five requirements.

1. **Revenue comes first.** The system must help acquire paying clients before unnecessary infrastructure is built.
2. **Evidence drives delivery.** Research, verification, and provenance must be integral to the workflow rather than optional quality checks.
3. **One accountable owner per task.** Multiple agents may contribute, but each task has one designated owner responsible for completion.
4. **Automate execution, not accountability.** Agents may perform approved work independently, but the founder retains authority over business commitments and consequential decisions.
5. **Build on the existing OS.** New capabilities should integrate with the Command Center rather than create duplicate databases and disconnected workflows.

# 2. The Four-Level Workforce

| Level | Organizational role | Production equivalent | Primary responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L4 | Producer / Decision Maker | Producer | Determines the outcome, identifies required work, creates the project plan, and establishes acceptance criteria. |
| L3 | Manager / Director | Director | Coordinates execution, delegates work, manages dependencies, monitors progress, and resolves operational issues within its authority. |
| L2 | Specialist Operator | Camera, audio, editor, specialist crew | Executes defined work using approved procedures, tools, and quality standards. |
| L1 | Assistant | Production assistant | Handles supporting tasks, information gathering, organization, and routine administrative work. |

The founder remains the ultimate business authority.

The L4 Producer makes project-level decisions within an approved objective and budget. It does not independently change the company's commercial commitments, authorize unrestricted expenditure, or override the founder's policies.

An AI agent may perform more than one organizational role initially. Separate agents should be introduced only when doing so improves reliability, specialization, or workload management.

# 3. Pipeline A — Market Intelligence and Business Development

**Objective:** Identify businesses that could benefit from LEGAiSEE and prioritize those most likely to become paying clients.

### Trigger

A scheduled prospecting cycle, a new market opportunity, a referral, or a founder-directed search.

### Workflow

1. The L4 Producer defines the target market, geographic area, business characteristics, and research objective.
2. The L3 Director converts the objective into a prospecting assignment.
3. An L2 Market Research Operator identifies potentially suitable businesses.
4. An L1 Research Assistant collects basic company information, public contact details, operating history, and relevant source links.
5. The Operator assembles a preliminary business profile.
6. The Director checks completeness and removes obvious duplicates.
7. The Producer evaluates whether the resulting prospect list satisfies the assignment.
8. Approved prospects enter the prospect-management pipeline.

### Required output

A structured prospect record containing, where available:

* Business name and website.
* Location and service area.
* Industry and business type.
* Approximate operating history.
* Ownership or generational history when publicly documented.
* Evidence of historical market presence.
* Potential authority-reconstruction opportunity.
* Relevant decision-maker and publicly available business contact information.
* Source links and research dates.
* Qualification status.
* Recommended next action.

### Automation policy

Research, extraction, deduplication, preliminary classification, and record creation are strong automation candidates.

Claims about company history must retain source references. An agent must not turn an unverified assumption into a factual claim merely because the claim sounds plausible.

**Founder involvement:** Approve the target market and commercial approach. Review prospecting quality until the process demonstrates consistent results.

# 4. Pipeline B — Qualification and Lead Management

**Objective:** Determine which prospects warrant further attention.

### Trigger

A prospect meets the initial screening criteria, responds to outreach, submits an inquiry, or arrives through a referral.

### Workflow

1. The L4 Producer defines the qualification criteria for the current service offering.
2. The L2 Qualification Operator reviews the prospect record and supporting evidence.
3. The Operator assesses historical depth, likely evidence availability, business relevance, apparent need, and access to decision-makers.
4. The Operator identifies missing information and unresolved assumptions.
5. The L1 Assistant prepares a concise qualification brief.
6. The L3 Director checks whether the brief is complete.
7. The Producer recommends one of four dispositions:

   * Advance.
   * Request additional information.
   * Defer.
   * Decline.
8. The founder approves exceptions and any consequential admission decisions.
9. The prospect record is updated with the disposition and next action.

### Required output

A qualification brief that explains:

* Why the prospect may be suitable.
* What problem LEGAiSEE might solve.
* What evidence supports that assessment.
* What remains unknown.
* Which service might be appropriate.
* What should happen next.

### Automation policy

AI can score prospects against explicit criteria and explain the evidence supporting its score.

It should not make the qualification score appear more precise than the underlying evidence warrants.

During launch, the founder should review qualification decisions until actual conversion and delivery data support more automated routing.

# 5. Pipeline C — Marketing and Authority Development

**Objective:** Create a repeatable flow of relevant prospects rather than relying entirely on individual outreach.

### Trigger

A scheduled publishing cycle, an identified market question, a campaign objective, or a documented insight worth sharing.

### Workflow

1. The Producer identifies the business objective and intended audience.
2. The Director creates a content assignment and distribution plan.
3. An L2 Research Operator gathers supporting evidence.
4. An L2 Content Operator develops the proposed asset.
5. An L1 Assistant checks formatting, links, metadata, and completeness.
6. The Director reviews the asset against the brief.
7. The founder approves public claims and external publication during the initial launch phase.
8. Approved material is published and distributed.
9. Performance information is collected.
10. The Producer evaluates results and adjusts subsequent assignments.

### Required output

A published asset or campaign accompanied by:

* Intended audience.
* Business objective.
* Supporting evidence where factual claims are made.
* Publication destination.
* Distribution plan.
* Performance measures.
* Follow-up actions.

### Automation policy

Research assistance, drafting, formatting, repurposing, scheduling, and performance reporting are potential automation candidates.

Public communications should not be generated indiscriminately. The system must preserve LEGAiSEE's positioning, protect confidential information, and avoid publishing unsupported historical or commercial claims.

# 6. Pipeline D — Outreach and Sales

**Objective:** Convert suitable prospects into paid engagements.

### Trigger

A qualified prospect is approved for outreach, an inquiry arrives, or a prospect requests a conversation.

### Workflow

1. The Producer determines the objective of the sales interaction.
2. The Research Operator prepares a prospect-specific briefing.
3. The Assistant assembles relevant correspondence, research, and meeting materials.
4. The Director verifies that the briefing is complete.
5. The founder conducts or approves consequential sales conversations.
6. The system records questions, needs, objections, next steps, and relevant deadlines.
7. The Producer recommends a suitable commission or assessment.
8. The Director coordinates preparation of the proposed scope, deliverables, timeline, and pricing.
9. The founder approves the commercial proposal.
10. The proposal is delivered through the approved communication process.
11. The system tracks the decision and any required follow-up.
12. A successful prospect moves into contracting and payment.

### Required output

A documented sales opportunity containing:

* Prospect qualification.
* Stated business problem.
* Proposed scope.
* Expected deliverables.
* Proposed timeline.
* Price and payment terms.
* Outstanding questions.
* Decision-maker.
* Current status.
* Next action and due date.

### Automation policy

Research, briefing preparation, proposal drafting, reminders, and CRM updates can be automated.

The founder retains authority over pricing exceptions, contractual commitments, and representations about what LEGAiSEE can deliver.

# 7. Pipeline E — Contracting, Payment, and Onboarding

**Objective:** Establish a valid, funded, clearly scoped engagement before substantive client work begins.

### Trigger

A prospect accepts a proposal in principle.

### Workflow

1. The Director checks that the proposed engagement has an approved scope.
2. The Assistant assembles the relevant onboarding materials.
3. The system prepares the appropriate agreement and payment request using approved templates.
4. The founder approves the agreement, commercial terms, and any exceptions.
5. The client receives the approved documents and payment instructions.
6. The payment system records the transaction status.
7. The system verifies payment through an authoritative payment-provider record.
8. The Director checks that required contractual and administrative prerequisites are satisfied.
9. The Assistant creates the engagement workspace and organizes the initial client records.
10. The client receives the onboarding instructions and initial information request.
11. The Director confirms that the engagement is ready to begin.
12. The project moves into excavation.

### Required output

A complete engagement record containing:

* Client and authorized contacts.
* Executed agreement.
* Approved scope and deliverables.
* Price and payment status.
* Start date and target milestones.
* Required client inputs.
* Access permissions.
* Confidentiality requirements.
* Project owner.
* Initial task plan.

### Automation policy

Document assembly, payment-status checks, workspace creation, reminders, and administrative recordkeeping are suitable automation candidates.

The system must not interpret a payment-page visit as proof of payment or treat an unsigned agreement as executed.

Substantive work begins only when the engagement's required contractual, payment, and onboarding conditions have been satisfied or the founder explicitly authorizes an exception.

# 8. Pipeline F — Evidence Acquisition and Excavation

**Objective:** Recover the historical artifacts and information required by the approved commission.

### Trigger

An engagement is approved for execution and the required initial client inputs are available.

### Workflow

1. The L4 Producer converts the commission scope into an evidence-acquisition plan.
2. The plan identifies research questions, relevant evidence classes, expected sources, and deliverable dependencies.
3. The L3 Director assigns acquisition tasks.
4. L2 Research Operators investigate approved public and client-authorized sources.
5. L1 Assistants collect metadata, organize files, and flag missing information.
6. Acquired artifacts are stored in the designated engagement workspace.
7. Each artifact receives an identifier and available provenance information.
8. The system records its source, acquisition date, processing status, and relevant permissions.
9. The Director checks whether the required evidence has been acquired.
10. Unavailable records and access barriers are documented.
11. The Producer determines whether the evidence is sufficient to proceed or whether the plan needs revision.
12. The founder approves material scope changes.

### Required output

An organized evidence inventory containing:

* Artifact identifier.
* Source and location.
* Acquisition date.
* File or record type.
* Relevant dates and entities.
* Provenance information.
* Access and use restrictions.
* Processing status.
* Relationship to the research question.
* Known limitations.

### Automation policy

File inventory, metadata extraction, text extraction, classification suggestions, and duplicate detection are strong automation candidates.

Agents must preserve original artifacts and distinguish source material from derived notes, interpretations, and generated summaries.

Access to private archives must be explicitly authorized.

# 9. Pipeline G — Evidence Processing and Verification

**Objective:** Convert acquired materials into traceable, defensible findings.

### Trigger

New artifacts are ingested or an existing finding requires additional verification.

### Workflow

1. The L4 Producer establishes the research questions and acceptance criteria.
2. An L2 Evidence Operator extracts relevant information from the artifacts.
3. The Operator proposes entities, dates, events, claims, and relationships.
4. The system links each extracted claim to its supporting source.
5. An L2 Verification Operator searches for independent corroboration.
6. The Operator records supporting, conflicting, and inconclusive evidence.
7. The system identifies potential duplicate sources and shared-source dependencies.
8. The Director checks completeness and routes unresolved issues for review.
9. Findings are classified according to the approved evidence hierarchy.
10. The system records confidence, limitations, and unresolved contradictions.
11. The Producer determines whether the finding satisfies the requirements for the next stage.
12. Material or ambiguous findings are escalated for human review.

### Required output

A set of traceable findings with:

* Claim or proposition.
* Supporting artifacts.
* Source independence assessment.
* Contradictory evidence.
* Evidence classification.
* Confidence assessment.
* Unresolved questions.
* Review status.
* Approval history.

### Automation policy

Extraction, entity resolution, candidate relationship discovery, source comparison, and contradiction detection can be assisted by AI.

Verification must not become circular. Three webpages repeating the same unsupported claim do not necessarily constitute three independent sources.

The exact verification requirements should follow the applicable LEGAiSEE methodology and the scope of the engagement.

**Quality rule:** A finding that fails its verification requirements remains unresolved. The system must not silently promote it to an established fact.

# 10. Pipeline H — Analysis and Authority Reconstruction

**Objective:** Translate verified evidence into a coherent reconstruction of the client's historical authority.

### Trigger

The engagement has sufficient verified evidence to begin the relevant analytical work.

### Workflow

1. The Producer identifies the analytical questions and required deliverables.
2. The Director divides the work into defined analytical assignments.
3. L2 specialist agents examine historical positioning, market presence, competitive distinctions, voice, institutional memory, and other relevant dimensions.
4. Each specialist documents its findings and links them to the evidence.
5. The Director coordinates cross-analysis and identifies inconsistencies.
6. The Producer synthesizes the findings into a proposed reconstruction.
7. The system distinguishes historical findings from present-day recommendations.
8. Quality-control checks identify unsupported assertions, missing evidence, and conflicts with the approved scope.
9. The founder reviews consequential interpretations and the proposed client-facing conclusions.
10. Approved findings move into deliverable production.

### Required output

A documented analytical package containing:

* Verified historical findings.
* Reconstructed positioning and authority signals.
* Supporting evidence.
* Relevant contradictions and limitations.
* Analytical conclusions.
* Proposed operational implications.
* Recommendations permitted by the engagement scope.

### Automation policy

AI can assist with cross-document synthesis, comparative analysis, thematic discovery, and drafting.

It must not manufacture historical certainty or present a plausible narrative as though it were proven.

# 11. Pipeline I — Deliverables, Review, and Client Acceptance

**Objective:** Produce and deliver the contracted work to the agreed standard.

### Trigger

The analytical package is ready for final production.

### Workflow

1. The Producer maps each contractual deliverable to its source findings.
2. The Director creates the production schedule.
3. L2 Production Operators prepare the required reports, dossiers, diagrams, and supporting materials.
4. L1 Assistants check formatting, file completeness, references, and document organization.
5. The Director verifies that all required deliverables are present.
6. The quality-control process checks factual traceability, internal consistency, and adherence to scope.
7. The founder approves final client-facing deliverables.
8. The client receives the deliverables through the approved delivery process.
9. Feedback and any acceptance requirements are recorded.
10. Requested corrections are assessed against the original scope.
11. Additional work is routed through an approved change process when necessary.
12. The engagement moves to closure or an agreed continuing service.

### Required output

A complete delivery record containing:

* Contracted deliverables.
* Final versions.
* Evidence references.
* Quality-control status.
* Approval history.
* Delivery date.
* Client feedback.
* Outstanding obligations.
* Closure or continuing-service status.

### Automation policy

Document assembly, formatting checks, reference validation, completeness checks, and version management can be automated.

Final approval remains with the founder until a later operating model explicitly delegates defined approval responsibilities.

# 12. Pipeline J — Retention and Continuing Authority Management

**Objective:** Maintain the value of the client's authority system and create appropriate opportunities for continued service.

### Trigger

A client engagement reaches completion, a retainer begins, a verification interval expires, or new relevant evidence emerges.

### Workflow

1. The Producer determines the continuing-service obligations defined by the agreement.
2. The Director creates the recurring task schedule.
3. Monitoring Operators identify relevant new information within the approved scope.
4. Verification Operators assess whether new information is sufficiently supported.
5. The system identifies changes, corrections, and potential contradictions.
6. The Director routes material findings for review.
7. The Producer recommends whether the client's record requires an update.
8. The founder or appropriately authorized reviewer approves material changes.
9. Approved updates are incorporated into the client record with version history.
10. The system records work performed against the continuing-service agreement.
11. Renewal dates and outstanding obligations are monitored.
12. The founder reviews renewal and expansion opportunities.

### Required output

A continuing-service record containing:

* Monitoring activity.
* New evidence.
* Verified changes.
* Corrections and addenda.
* Version history.
* Work completed.
* Remaining obligations.
* Renewal dates.
* Recommended next actions.

### Automation policy

Scheduled monitoring, source checks, reminder generation, and change detection are potential automation candidates.

A continuing-service agreement must specify what is monitored, how frequently it is reviewed, and what the client receives. The system must not imply unlimited monitoring or guaranteed detection of every relevant development.

# 13. Pipeline K — Finance and Administration

**Objective:** Maintain accurate financial records and ensure that operational commitments are visible and controlled.

### Trigger

A financial transaction, contractual obligation, recurring expense, reporting deadline, or administrative event occurs.

### Workflow

1. The system records the transaction or obligation.
2. The Assistant collects the associated invoice, receipt, agreement, or supporting record.
3. The system categorizes the item for bookkeeping review.
4. The Director identifies missing documentation and outstanding actions.
5. The founder approves expenditures and financial commitments within the applicable approval policy.
6. Payment records are reconciled against the payment provider and bank records.
7. Outstanding invoices and receivables are monitored.
8. Cash-flow information is updated.
9. Required financial records are organized for the appropriate reporting period.
10. Exceptions are escalated for human review or specialist assistance.

### Required output

An accurate operational financial record containing:

* Revenue and payment status.
* Outstanding receivables.
* Expenses and supporting documentation.
* Recurring obligations.
* Cash-flow outlook.
* Reconciliation status.
* Required follow-up actions.

### Automation policy

Transaction imports, receipt organization, reminders, preliminary categorization, and routine reporting can be automated.

Financial transfers, tax filings, accounting judgments, and other consequential actions require the appropriate authorization or qualified professional review.

# 14. Pipeline L — Internal Technology and System Operations

**Objective:** Keep the LEGAiSEE operating environment reliable, secure, and recoverable.

### Trigger

A scheduled maintenance cycle, software change, detected error, security event, backup event, or failed workflow.

### Workflow

1. The Director checks operational status and outstanding incidents.
2. Monitoring tools identify failures, errors, and overdue tasks.
3. The Assistant assembles diagnostic information.
4. The appropriate Operator investigates the affected subsystem.
5. The Producer evaluates the impact on business operations.
6. Changes are tested in an appropriate environment before deployment.
7. The founder approves consequential changes to production systems.
8. The system records the change, its purpose, and its result.
9. Backups and recovery procedures are checked according to the established policy.
10. Incidents are documented and tracked through resolution.

### Required output

An operational record containing:

* System status.
* Detected incidents.
* Affected modules.
* Diagnostic evidence.
* Changes made.
* Test results.
* Backup status.
* Recovery status.
* Outstanding risks.

### Automation policy

Monitoring, scheduled checks, diagnostic collection, and routine notifications are good automation candidates.

Agents should not independently deploy unreviewed changes to critical production systems, alter security controls, or delete source evidence.

# 15. Cross-Pipeline Rules

Every pipeline must follow the same basic operating contract.

## Task contract

Each task should define:

1. Task identifier.
2. Objective.
3. Responsible owner.
4. Required inputs.
5. Expected output.
6. Acceptance criteria.
7. Applicable permissions.
8. Dependencies.
9. Deadline or scheduling rule.
10. Error and escalation procedure.
11. Completion evidence.

## Handoff contract

A handoff is not complete merely because one agent says it has finished.

The receiving role must be able to identify the output, inspect its status, and determine whether the next step can proceed.

Where verification is required, the handoff must include the evidence supporting the result.

## Exception handling

Agents must escalate when:

* Required information is unavailable.
* Evidence conflicts materially.
* A task exceeds its authorized scope.
* A required action cannot be completed.
* A financial or contractual exception arises.
* Confidentiality or security may be compromised.
* The system detects a potentially consequential error.

## Auditability

Material work should leave a record of what was done, by whom or which agent, using which inputs, and with what result.

For research findings, the record must preserve source provenance. For financial and administrative actions, it must preserve the appropriate transaction or approval record.

# 16. Initial Implementation Priorities

The complete architecture is the target state. It is not the launch checklist.

The initial implementation should prioritize the following.

### Priority 1 — Protect the system

* Verify authentication and authorization.
* Review public exposure of development information.
* Suppress sensitive server-side errors from public pages.
* Verify client-data isolation.
* Confirm backup and recovery procedures.
* Reconcile payment configuration before accepting real payments.

### Priority 2 — Establish a minimum revenue pipeline

* Prospect records.
* Qualification workflow.
* Outreach and follow-up tracking.
* Sales opportunity records.
* Proposal and agreement process.
* Payment verification.
* Basic client onboarding.

### Priority 3 — Establish a minimum delivery pipeline

* Engagement workspace.
* Artifact inventory.
* Evidence provenance.
* Research and verification workflow.
* Deliverable tracking.
* Human review and approval.
* Secure client delivery.

### Priority 4 — Introduce AI execution

* Start with bounded research and administrative tasks.
* Add structured task inputs and outputs.
* Introduce a Director workflow for coordinating tasks.
* Add the Producer workflow for converting approved objectives into project plans.
* Log actions and test failure handling.
* Expand autonomy only when performance is demonstrated.

### Priority 5 — Add scale capabilities

* Recurring monitoring.
* Retention and renewal workflows.
* Advanced analysis and graph operations.
* More sophisticated agent coordination.
* Expanded reporting and performance analytics.

# 17. Completion Standard

The operating architecture is ready for implementation when every material business activity has:

* A defined trigger.
* An accountable role.
* An expected output.
* An established quality standard.
* An appropriate tool or system.
* An explicit authority boundary.
* A documented exception procedure.

The next phase is to convert this pipeline architecture into a workload model and a minimum viable staffing configuration.

That work will determine which roles should initially be combined, which AI capabilities should be implemented first, and which functions should remain manual until revenue justifies further investment.
