LEGAiSEE CINEMATIC WEBSITE
PRODUCTION MASTER BRIEF — V1.0
Status: PRE-PRODUCTION / GOVERNING HANDOFF
 Parent Document: LEGAiSEE Cinematic Website Governance Doctrine v1.0
 Purpose: Translate the Doctrine into an operational brief for the AI creative/technical production process.

1. PROJECT IDENTITY
Project: LEGAiSEE Public Website
 Domain: legaisee.com
 Experience Type: Interactive Cinematic Website
 Medium: Browser
 Primary Audience: Prospective LEGAiSEE clients and strategic decision-makers
 Primary Objective: Establish LEGAiSEE as an unusually authoritative, sophisticated, evidence-driven strategic practice and move qualified visitors toward engagement.
Core proposition
LEGAiSEE is not simply presenting information.
It is presenting a way of seeing.
The website therefore needs to make the visitor experience the underlying LEGAiSEE philosophy rather than merely read a description of it.

2. THE CREATIVE NORTH STAR
The website should feel like:
A brand film transferred from camera to code.
The viewer should feel as though they are moving through a carefully directed cinematic environment.
The scrollbar functions as the playhead.
The camera, environment, typography, objects, lighting, and information all respond to that playhead.
The experience should feel authored rather than generated.

3. THE VIEWER'S MENTAL MODEL
The intended mental model is:
"I am looking through a camera, and scrolling is allowing me to move through the film."
The viewer should not primarily perceive:
webpage sections
animation triggers
CSS effects
3D demos
individual widgets
Instead they should perceive:
movement
framing
discovery
progression
spatial relationships
information emerging
environments changing
ideas connecting

4. WHAT THIS WEBSITE IS NOT
The production must actively avoid becoming:
A generic luxury website
Black + gold + serif typography + huge whitespace is insufficient.
A 3D showcase
3D exists only when it contributes to cinematography.
A videogame
No unnecessary environments, controls, avatars, HUDs, game-like interfaces, or gratuitous 3D objects.
A video website
The primary cinematic experience must not be a pre-rendered video masquerading as scroll interaction.
A parallax template
Independent foreground/background movement alone does not satisfy the concept.
A collection of animation tricks
Effects must serve narrative, information, composition, or transition.
An application
The public website is not the LEGAiSEE operating interface.

6. THE CENTRAL METAPHOR
The existing LEGAiSEE philosophy naturally supports a cinematic progression:
Something important exists beneath the surface.
The visitor begins with incomplete understanding.
Through movement and revelation:
surface → evidence → excavation → relationship → structure → signal → authority
The website should investigate that progression visually.
This does not mean every scene literally needs dirt, rocks, archaeology, or digging.
The metaphor should evolve beyond the literal.

Conceptually:

       ↓

       +

11. SCENE MODEL
Every major public page/concept is treated as a cinematic scene.
Each scene can contain:
SCENE
 ├── Environment
 ├── Camera
 ├── Objects
 ├── Typography
 ├── Lighting
 ├── Information
 ├── Motion
 ├── Timeline
 ├── Transition In
 └── Transition Out
A scene may contain multiple cinematic shots.

12. SCENE PRODUCTION RULE
Every scene must first be understood creatively before it is implemented technically.
The process is:
WHAT DOES THIS SCENE MEAN?
          ↓
WHAT SHOULD THE VIEWER EXPERIENCE?
          ↓
WHAT SHOULD THEY SEE?
          ↓
WHAT SHOULD MOVE?
          ↓
WHAT SHOULD THE CAMERA DO?
          ↓
WHAT SHOULD BE REVEALED?
          ↓
WHAT TECHNIQUE CAN PRODUCE THAT?
Not:
WHAT CODE CAN WE WRITE?
          ↓
WHAT CAN WE ANIMATE?
          ↓
WHY DOES IT EXIST?

16. AI CREATIVE ROLE
AI is expected to perform the majority of exploratory work.
AI should:
Analyze the existing site.
Identify its narrative structure.
Identify opportunities for cinematic interpretation.
Develop multiple possible creative directions where useful.
Select/recommend the strongest direction.
Explain why it fits LEGAiSEE.
Develop scene concepts.
Design camera choreography.
Design transitions.
Determine appropriate asset strategies.
Determine technical requirements.
Prototype the experience.
Present it for Director review.
Revise based on feedback.
The user should not have to manually storyboard every frame before the process can begin.

17. HUMAN DIRECTOR ROLE
The Director provides:
business intent
creative taste
approval
rejection
qualitative direction
final visual judgment
brand judgment
The Director does not need to specify every implementation detail.
Useful direction may be as simple as:
"Too much."
"Too game-like."
"Camera needs to feel heavier."
"That reveal is excellent."
"Typography needs more authority."
"This feels like a template."
AI then determines the technical and creative implications while preserving the Doctrine.

18. ASSET STRATEGY
The production should maximize:
cinematic richness per byte.
Use browser-generated techniques where appropriate.
Prefer generated/code-driven:
typography
gradients
simple geometry
lines
lighting
shadows
camera motion
spatial transforms
particles
masks
simple materials
transitions
Use optimized assets for:
photography
unique environments
unique artwork
complex textures
visual information that cannot be economically recreated

19. AFTER EFFECTS RELATIONSHIP
Adobe/After Effects experience remains highly valuable.
AE should be treated as a creative reference and asset-production environment, not necessarily the browser's rendering engine.
The desired workflow is:
Think like an editor/cinematographer → design the composition → translate its behavior into browser-native motion.
Do not export every AE layer simply because it exists as an AE layer.

20. VIDEO RULE
The website's primary cinematic movement should be generated interactively.
Do not solve the cinematic problem by rendering an entire sequence to video and putting a scroll controller around it.
The viewer should be controlling a scene state, not merely controlling video playback.

21. PERFORMANCE STRATEGY
Performance must be considered during scene design.
Every scene should eventually have an asset/render budget appropriate to its importance.
Questions include:
What must be loaded immediately?
What can be deferred?
What can be procedural?
What can be compressed?
What can be reused?
What can be removed without changing the experience?
What happens on mobile?
What happens on lower-powered devices?

22. RESPONSIVE STRATEGY
Desktop is not automatically the canonical composition.
The cinematic intention is canonical.
The implementation may differ by viewport.
A mobile scene may use:
different framing
reduced depth
fewer objects
altered camera path
simplified effects
modified timing
alternate typography
different transitions
while preserving the same narrative purpose.

23. ACCESSIBILITY STRATEGY
The experience must retain usable content independent of cinematic effects.
The visitor must still be able to:
navigate
read
understand
identify links
access content
use controls
Reduced motion should provide a coherent alternate presentation rather than simply destroying the experience.

24. SEO / WEB INTEGRITY
Cinematic behavior must not require abandoning conventional web fundamentals.
Where appropriate:
pages retain meaningful URLs
content remains indexable
navigation remains understandable
deep links remain possible
browser history remains functional
semantic content remains available
The cinematic layer enhances the website.
It does not replace its fundamental web identity.

29. REGRESSION RULE
Every consequential modification requires evaluation beyond the modified element.
Minimum mental model:
ELEMENT
   ↓
SHOT
   ↓
SCENE
   ↓
PREVIOUS SCENE
   ↓
NEXT SCENE
   ↓
GLOBAL SYSTEM
The larger the change, the larger the regression scope.

30. CHANGE CONTROL

31. STOP CONDITIONS
AI must stop and surface the issue when:
a requested change contradicts the Doctrine
a locked scene must be substantially altered
a local fix requires system-level modification
a technical shortcut compromises the intended experience
performance becomes unacceptable
a proposed asset strategy becomes excessive
a change affects unrelated scenes
existing architecture is insufficiently understood
the request changes the fundamental visual identity
the final vision cannot be preserved

34. PRODUCTION DISCIPLINE
The project must resist three recurring failure modes.
Failure Mode 1 — Tunnel Vision
Fixing one thing without understanding its system consequences.
Countermeasure: Macro-first governance.
Failure Mode 2 — Technology Drift
Choosing the visual direction because a technology makes something possible.
Countermeasure: Creative requirement precedes technology.
Failure Mode 3 — Effect Inflation
Adding more effects because the experience doesn't yet feel impressive.
Countermeasure: Improve cinematography, composition, pacing, and narrative before adding complexity.

35. THE MASTER PRODUCTION QUESTION
At every stage:
What is the viewer supposed to experience here, and how does that experience contribute to the complete LEGAiSEE film?
That question precedes implementation.
