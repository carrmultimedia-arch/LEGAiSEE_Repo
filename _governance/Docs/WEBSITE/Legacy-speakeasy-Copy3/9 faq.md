FAQ — SPEAKEASY VERSION

Most of the questions people have don’t show up right away.
They come up somewhere between reading…
 and deciding whether to reach out.
This tends to cover the ones that matter.

How do I know if this even applies to my business?
Usually, you already have a sense.
If you’ve been operating for a few years,
 if you’ve created content, run campaigns, tried different directions—
there’s almost always something there.
Not everything is worth recovering.
But most businesses aren’t starting from nothing.
They’re starting from something they stopped using.
The survey is what confirms that.

What do you actually mean by “verified”?
It means nothing gets used just because it looks right.
If something shows up—an idea, a campaign, a pattern—
 it gets checked against other signals.
Different sources. Different angles.
If it holds up, it stays.
If it doesn’t, it gets ignored.
That’s what keeps this from turning into guesswork.

What about sensitive information?
Nothing gets exposed.
Most of what’s useful is already sitting in places you control—
 platforms, files, past work, internal material.
When something needs to be handled more carefully, it is.
Quietly. Directly. Without it moving anywhere it shouldn’t.
This only works if trust is intact.

Why bring AI or coaching into this at all?
They’re not the starting point.
They come in later.
Once something real has been uncovered,
 it can be expanded, structured, or communicated more effectively.
AI helps with scale and refinement.
Coaching comes into play if you’re the one stepping forward—
 speaking, presenting, becoming more visible.
But none of that replaces the core work.
It builds on it.

How quickly does this turn into results?
Depends on how far you go.
At the surface level, things show up quickly.
You might see something within days that changes how you think about what you’ve done.
Deeper work takes longer.
Not because it’s slow—
 but because accuracy matters more than speed.
When something is reconstructed properly, it doesn’t just spike.
It holds.

Is this only for larger, established businesses?
No.
But there does need to be something to work with.
Time in operation helps.
So does having created, tested, or published consistently.
The more history there is, the more there is to uncover.
But even smaller businesses often have more beneath the surface than they expect.

Most of these questions tend to answer themselves
 once you take the first step and actually look.
Until then, they stay theoretical.

If you’re unsure, that’s normal.
That’s usually where this starts.
