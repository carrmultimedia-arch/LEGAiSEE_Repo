PRICING / INVESTMENT — SPEAKEASY VERSION

Most people come here looking for numbers.
That’s fair.
But if pricing is the first thing that makes sense,
 the rest of this probably won’t.
Because nothing here is priced like a service.
It’s priced based on how deep the excavation goes.

At the surface level, not much is required.
A light survey.
 A quick orientation.
Just enough to determine whether anything of value is actually there.
That’s where most engagements begin.

If there is something worth exploring—and there usually is—
 the next step isn’t a full commitment.
It’s a controlled dig.
Focused. Limited. Precise.
Enough to recover something real.
Not a theory. Not a recommendation.
Something that already existed…
 but wasn’t being used.
That stage tends to change how people see their own business.
Because it replaces assumptions with evidence.

From there, the decision becomes clearer.
Not “should we invest more?”
But:
“How much is actually sitting underneath this?”

A full excavation is where the real work happens.
This is where everything gets pulled into view.
Not just content or campaigns,
 but structure, timing, decisions—
 the full sequence of how things evolved.
What worked.
 What didn’t.
 What was abandoned too early.
 What was never fully developed.
It’s methodical.
And it’s not fast.
Because accuracy matters more than speed at this level.

Once something valuable has been recovered,
 the focus shifts.
Less about discovery.
More about making sure it holds.
What’s been uncovered gets restored, organized, and reassembled
 into something that can actually function in the present.
And then… protected.
Because without that, the same patterns repeat.
Things drift.
Signals weaken.
And eventually, the same value gets buried again.

For some, that’s enough.
For others, there’s a final layer.
Where what’s been rebuilt doesn’t just operate quietly in the background—
 it becomes visible.
Structured.
Intentional.
Aligned with how the business (and sometimes the individual behind it)
 is meant to be seen.

If you’re looking for ranges, they exist.
A survey-level engagement typically sits around $1,000–$1,500.
Initial excavation work expands from there.
A full, primary excavation—where everything is recovered and reconstructed—
 generally falls between $5,000 and $8,500, depending on scope.
Ongoing preservation, where everything is maintained and refined over time,
 typically operates between $2,000 and $3,500 per month.
And for those stepping into a more visible role—
 where the work extends beyond the business and into personal authority—
 those engagements begin around $7,500 and scale upward based on involvement.

Those numbers aren’t arbitrary.
They reflect:
how much history needs to be reconstructed
how fragmented things have become
how much value is recoverable
Some businesses require very little.
Others are sitting on years of untapped material.

That’s why everything starts the same way.
Light.
Controlled.
Just enough to see what’s there.

From that point, the path tends to reveal itself.
Not because it’s being sold.
Because it becomes obvious.

There’s no pressure to go further than that.
But most people don’t expect what they find when they take the first step.
