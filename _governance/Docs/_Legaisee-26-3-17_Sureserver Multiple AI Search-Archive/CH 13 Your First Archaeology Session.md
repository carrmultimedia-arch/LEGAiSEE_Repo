CHAPTER 13: Your First Archaeology Session
13.1 Preparing a Research Query
What this is:
The process of formulating a clear, specific research question that can be effectively posed to multiple AI platforms simultaneously, with consideration for how the responses will be compared and synthesized.
Where this exists in the real world:
Think of a journalist preparing interview questions. A vague question ("Tell me about the economy") produces vague answers. A specific question ("How did the 2023 interest rate hikes affect small business lending in Texas?") produces actionable intelligence. Your query preparation follows the same discipline.
How you encounter this:
You will learn to craft queries that: (1) are specific enough to produce comparable answers, (2) are open-ended enough to allow different AI strengths to emerge, (3) include context that all platforms can interpret, and (4) are structured for easy extraction of key insights.
What this controls or affects:
Query quality determines capture value. Poor queries waste excavation time and produce uncomparable responses. Well-crafted queries yield rich, contrastable intelligence that justifies the multi-AI approach.
What goes wrong when this is misunderstood:
Too broad → responses diverge completely, no comparison possible
Too narrow → all platforms give identical answers, no value in multi-AI
Ambiguous terms → each platform interprets differently unintentionally
No extraction plan → captures sit unread, value unrealized
Concept Lock-In:
Multi-AI excavation is comparative intelligence gathering. The value is not in getting one answer, but in seeing how different systems approach the same question. Your query must be a lens that reveals differences in reasoning, knowledge, and presentation.
CLICK-BY-CLICK: Crafting Your First Research Query
Step 1: Open Your Research Notebook
Create a document (physical or digital) titled "Archaeology Session Log." This is where you record your query, rationale, and synthesis notes.
Step 2: Define Your Information Need
Write down in plain language what you need to know. Example:
"I need to understand the current state of AI regulation in the European Union, specifically how the AI Act will affect small software companies building recommendation systems. I need to know compliance timelines, penalties, and practical implementation steps."
Step 3: Refine to Specific Query
Convert your need into a query suitable for AI platforms:
Draft 1 (too broad): "Tell me about EU AI regulation."
Draft 2 (too narrow): "What is Article 14 of the EU AI Act?"
Final Query:
"Explain the EU AI Act's requirements for recommendation systems used by small software companies (<50 employees). Cover: (1) which risk category applies, (2) specific compliance requirements, (3) implementation timeline from 2024-2026, (4) penalties for non-compliance, and (5) practical first steps for a company with no current compliance infrastructure."
Step 4: Structure for Comparison
Add explicit structure that all platforms will follow:
*"Please organize your response with these sections:
RISK CLASSIFICATION (which category and why)
COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS (bullet points)
TIMELINE (dates and milestones)
PENALTIES (fines and other consequences)
ACTION STEPS (prioritized list for small companies)"*
Step 5: Prepare Context Document
Create a text file with:
plainCopy
ARCHAEOLOGY SESSION: EU AI Act Research
Date: [Today's date]
Business Context: Client consulting project
Query: [Your final query]

Expected Platforms: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity
Synthesis Goal: Create compliance roadmap for client presentation
Key Elements to Compare: Risk classification, timeline, action steps
Save this as session-prep-2026-03-16.txt in your working folder.
What this controls or affects:
You now have a query designed for multi-AI excavation. The structure ensures comparable responses. The context document preserves your intent for later synthesis.
What goes wrong when this is misunderstood:
Forgetting business context → captures lack metadata, harder to find later
No synthesis goal → read responses but extract no actionable intelligence
Platform assumptions → some platforms (Perplexity) excel at research, others (Claude) at analysis—your query should leverage both
Concept Lock-In:
Query preparation is intellectual labor that multiplies output quality. Time spent here saves 10x time in synthesis. The Archaeology system captures efficiently, but cannot think for you. Your structured query is the difference between data collection and intelligence gathering.

13.3 Capturing Responses
What this is:
The process of extracting and archiving the AI-generated responses once they are complete, using either individual capture buttons or the coordinated "Capture All" function.
Where this exists in the real world:
Think of a court reporter transcribing testimony. The words are spoken (AI generates response), the reporter records them (you capture), and the record is preserved (archive file created). Your capture is the transcription moment.
How you encounter this:
You will wait for all responses to complete, verify each contains substantial content, then use the Archaeology extension to capture all four responses simultaneously with session correlation.
CLICK-BY-CLICK: Capturing Your First Multi-AI Responses
Step 1: Wait for Response Completion
Monitor all four windows. Completion indicators vary by platform:
ChatGPT: "Regenerate" button appears, cursor stops blinking
Claude: Response ends with no "..." indicator, send button active
Gemini: Response complete, no loading spinner
Perplexity: Sources listed at bottom, no "searching" indicator
Typical timing:
Perplexity: 5-15 seconds (fastest, searches web)
ChatGPT: 10-30 seconds
Claude: 15-45 seconds (often most thorough)
Gemini: 10-30 seconds
Wait until all four show complete responses.
Step 2: Verify Response Quality
Quickly scan each response for:
✅ Actually addresses your query (not off-topic)
✅ Contains substantial content (not just "I cannot answer that")
✅ Structure matches your requested format (if specified)
If any platform failed or gave poor response, note it in your log:

- Gemini: ⚠️ Off-topic, will exclude from synthesis
- Perplexity: ✅ Good with sources

What this controls or affects:
Your research session is now preserved in your archive. The three responses are linked by session ID, enabling you to retrieve them as a group for synthesis. The capture process took approximately 30 seconds for 3 platforms.
What goes wrong when this is misunderstood:
Capturing before response complete → truncated content in archive
Window closed before capture → capture fails for that platform
Network interruption during capture → partial session, some platforms missing
Not waiting for "success" confirmation → assumed capture that failed
Concept Lock-In:
Capture is preservation of transient intelligence. AI responses are ephemeral—refresh the page and they may change (especially with model updates). Your archive creates permanent, referenceable records. The session correlation preserves the temporal relationship—"these were answers to the same question at the same time."

How you encounter this:
You will read all three captured responses side-by-side (or sequentially), note differences in risk classification, timeline interpretations, and action recommendations, then create a synthesis document that combines the best insights from each platform.
