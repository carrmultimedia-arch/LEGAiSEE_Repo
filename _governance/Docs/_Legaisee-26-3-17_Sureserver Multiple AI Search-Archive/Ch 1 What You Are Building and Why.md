PART I: FOUNDATION

CHAPTER 1: What You Are Building and Why
1.1 The Problem: Scattered AI Conversations
What this is:
You use multiple AI platforms (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity) for research. Each conversation contains valuable insights about business archaeology—finding old marketing assets, analyzing competitors, developing strategies. But these conversations are scattered across different websites, trapped in each platform's interface, with no easy way to search across them all or connect insights from different AIs.
Where this exists in the real world:
This problem exists in every knowledge worker's daily workflow. Researchers, consultants, strategists, and analysts all face the same challenge: valuable conversations locked in silos.
How you encounter this:
You ask ChatGPT a question, then ask Claude the same question to compare answers. Later, you remember ChatGPT said something useful but you can't find it. You have 47 ChatGPT conversations and no way to search them all at once. Meanwhile, your Claude conversations are in a different place entirely.
What this controls or affects:
This controls your ability to:
Build cumulative knowledge over time
Find insights you previously discovered
Compare AI perspectives on the same topic
Create reusable research assets
What goes wrong when this is misunderstood:
Most people either:
Accept that AI conversations are disposable (losing valuable work)
Try to manually copy/paste everything (wasting hours)
Subscribe to expensive third-party tools they don't fully control
Never build a personal knowledge base from their AI research
Concept Lock-In:
AI conversations are valuable intellectual work product. They deserve to be captured, organized, searchable, and permanently owned by you—not locked inside corporate platforms.

1.2 The Solution: Your Personal Archaeology Platform
What this is:
A system you build and control that:
Captures conversations from multiple AI platforms automatically
Stores them on your own server (SureServer) in organized folders
Provides a clean, Notion-like dashboard for browsing and searching
Lets you launch research sessions across multiple AIs simultaneously
Organizes everything by "Raw" (unprocessed), "Cut" (in review), and "Polished" (finished intelligence)
Where this exists in the real world:
This is similar to personal knowledge management systems like Obsidian or Notion, but specifically designed for AI research workflows and business archaeology.
How you encounter this:
When complete, your daily workflow becomes: open dashboard → launch multi-AI research → capture valuable responses → find any past research instantly → build cumulative business intelligence.
What this controls or affects:
This controls:
Your research efficiency (find past work in seconds, not hours)
Your intellectual property ownership (you own the archive, not AI companies)
Your competitive advantage (cumulative knowledge vs. starting fresh each time)
What goes wrong when this is misunderstood:
Building this requires understanding that:
You are creating infrastructure, not just a website
The value compounds over time (empty at first, invaluable after 6 months)
Simplicity beats features (better to have 3 working features than 10 broken ones)
Concept Lock-In:
You are building an intelligence archive—a personal system for capturing, organizing, and retrieving AI-assisted research. This is infrastructure for your business archaeology methodology.

1.4 The Three-Layer Architecture
Layer 1: The Dashboard (Your Command Center)
Location: Your SureServer (yourdomain.com/archaeology/)
Technology: HTML, CSS, JavaScript (all free, standard web)
Purpose: Browse archives, launch research sessions, review captures
Layer 2: The Browser Extension (Your Field Tool)
Location: Installed in Chrome/Edge
Technology: JavaScript, browser APIs
Purpose: Capture chats, launch arranged windows, inject buttons into AI sites
Layer 3: The Archive Engine (Your Library)
Location: Your SureServer (files + email processing)
Technology: PHP, file system, email IMAP
Purpose: Store chats permanently, organize automatically, process incoming data
