CHAPTER 27: The Complete Discovery Call & Proposal System
Overview
This chapter contains the exact words to say, the precise documents to send, and the specific follow-up sequence to convert interested prospects into paying clients. No improvisation required. Follow the script, use the templates, execute the sequence.
The Promise: After mastering this system, you will consistently convert 40–60% of discovery calls into Stage 1 Audit clients, with a sales process that feels like consultation, not persuasion.

Part 1: The Discovery Call Script (Word-for-Word)
Call Duration: 30 minutes
Your Goal: Secure a "yes" to the Stage 1 Audit or schedule a specific follow-up
Mindset: You are the diagnostician, not the salesperson. Your job is to uncover truth, not convince.

PRE-CALL PREPARATION (15 minutes before)
Research Checklist:
[ ] Business name, website, years in operation confirmed
[ ] 5-Point Excavation Summary drafted (Chapter 20)
[ ] Decision-maker status confirmed (or plan to confirm in opening)
[ ] Pricing tier determined (Foundation $2,000 / Elevation $3,500 / Premium $5,000+)
[ ] Calendar open for next 14 days (if they say yes)
[ ] Proposal template ready to customize and send
Materials Open:
Their website (reviewed)
Wayback Machine (checked for historical snapshots)
YouTube (searched "[business] commercial")
LinkedIn (found owner/decision-maker profile)
Your CRM or notes (previous touchpoints logged)

PHASE 1: OPENING — 0 to 2 minutes
Purpose: Establish authority, confirm decision-maker status, set agenda
Exact Words:
"Thanks for taking the call, [Name]. Before we dive in—are you the person who makes marketing decisions for [Company], or should I also include someone else in this conversation?"
If NOT decision-maker:
"Who else should be involved if we decide to move forward? I'd prefer to have everyone on the same call rather than play telephone. Should we reschedule with [Name] included, or can you brief me now and we'll loop them in later?"
If decision-maker:
"Good. I have 30 minutes blocked. Here's how I'd like to use it: I'll share what I found in my 20 minutes of research, then I'd love to hear the history from your perspective. Sound good?"
Why this works: You are qualifying them, not the reverse. The question about decision-makers prevents the "I need to check with my partner" stall at the end.

PHASE 2: THE RESEARCH REVEAL — 2 to 8 minutes
Purpose: Demonstrate competence, create curiosity, invite correction
Exact Words:
"I spent 20 minutes researching [Company] before this call. Here's what I found:"
Deliver the 5-Point Excavation Summary (60 seconds per point):
Point 1: The Asset
"The Asset: I found [specific finding with date and detail]. [Describe what you found: an old campaign, a previous website, a notable tactic, an award, a specific era of success]. This appears to have been active approximately [years]."
Point 2: The Abandonment
"The Abandonment: This approach stopped around [year]. From what I can see, it was replaced by [what came next, or nothing]. The transition seems to have coincided with [event, trend, or leadership change if known]."
Point 3: The Current State
"The Current State: Today, [neutral, factual observation of current marketing]. [Website, social media, recent campaigns—what you see now]."
Point 4: The Competitive Gap
"The Competitive Gap: While [Company] moved away from [old tactic], [Competitor A] and [Competitor B] appear to have doubled down on similar approaches. [Specific example of what competitors are doing now]."
Point 5: The Question
"The Question: This raises a question I'm hoping you can help me with: [Company] clearly had something that worked in [era]. What would happen if that approach were excavated, modernized, and redeployed today? Would it still resonate with your market, or has that opportunity passed?"
Pause. Wait for response.
Why this works: You have demonstrated deep research in 5 minutes. You have shown you understand their history better than they expect. You have asked a genuine question that invites them to think strategically, not defensively.

PHASE 3: THE HISTORY EXTRACTION — 8 to 18 minutes
Purpose: Let them tell the story, uncover emotional drivers, identify specific pain
Exact Words:
"What am I missing? What did I get wrong?"
Listen actively. Take notes. Probe with these questions:
If they confirm your findings:
"Tell me more about [era identified]. What was working then? Who was running marketing? What did customers say about you?"
If they correct you:
"I appreciate that correction. So what actually happened was...?"
If they seem surprised by your findings:
"You didn't know about [asset you found]? Let me show you... [Share screen if virtual, or describe]. How does that land?"
Deepening Questions (use 3–4 based on their responses):
"Who was running marketing at that time?"
"What did customers say about you that made you proud?"
"Why did that approach stop? Was it a deliberate decision or did it just fade?"
"What replaced it? Did that work better, or was it just different?"
"If you could bring back one thing from that era, what would it be?"
"How do you feel when you look at what [Competitor A] is doing now?"
"What's the story you tell yourself about why [Company] isn't where it used to be?"
"What have you tried in the last 2 years to turn things around?"
Listen for emotional language:
Frustration: "We just can't seem to..."
Nostalgia: "Those were the days when..."
Fear: "I'm worried that..."
Hope: "I keep thinking if we could just..."
Why this works: People buy based on emotion and justify with logic. This phase surfaces the emotional stakes. You are not selling yet; you are understanding.

PHASE 4: THE DIAGNOSIS — 18 to 24 minutes
Purpose: Name the pattern, introduce your methodology, present the solution
Exact Words:
"I'm hearing a pattern. [Summarize in one sentence: Golden era → Abandonment → Current struggle]."
Pattern Examples:
"You dominated [channel] in [era], abandoned it for [new trend], and now [competitor] owns that space."
"You had a clear message in [era], got distracted by [tactic], and now your positioning is muddy."
"You built trust through [method], stopped when [event happened], and now you're competing on price."
"This is common—businesses abandon working tactics before their time, often chasing trends that don't fit their DNA. The good news: [Specific tactic] worked because of [psychology], not because of [obsolete technology]. That psychology still works—it just needs modern execution."
Transition to your methodology:
"What I do is excavate those proven assets, modernize them for today's platforms, and build systems to deploy them repeatedly. I call it Business Archaeology."
Introduce the Stage 1 Audit:
"For [Company], I'd want to do a full Business History Audit. That means:
Deep excavation across 40+ channels (not just what I found in 20 minutes)
15–25 specific assets documented with detail
Analysis of what made them work psychologically
Modernization recommendations for today's platforms
10-page report plus video walkthrough
Delivered in 5–7 business days"
Present investment:
"The investment is $[2,000/3,500/5,000], delivered in 5–7 business days."
Why this works: You have diagnosed their problem in terms they recognize. You have positioned your solution as the logical response to their specific situation. The price is stated as fact, not request.

PHASE 5: THE CLOSE — 24 to 30 minutes
Purpose: Secure commitment or schedule specific next step
Exact Words:
"Does that make sense for where [Company] is right now?"
IF YES—Immediate Close:
"Great. I can start [Monday/Wednesday]. I'll send the proposal and invoice now—payment locks your spot. Any questions before I send it?"
Handle questions:
"What's included?" → Reference the list you just gave
"How does payment work?" → "50% to begin, 50% on delivery. I can send the invoice right after this call."
"What do you need from me?" → "Access to any old marketing files you have, and 60 minutes of your time for a deeper interview."
IF HESITANT—Address Concerns:
"What specific questions do you need answered?"
Common hesitations and responses:
"I need to think about it."
"Of course. What's your biggest hesitation? Let me address it directly."
If price: "What would need to be true for this to be worth $[amount] to you?"
If timing: "What would change between now and [specific date]?"
If uncertainty: "What would you need to see to feel confident this is the right move?"
IF "Need to check with partner":
"Absolutely. When could you and [Name] talk? I'd rather schedule a brief follow-up with both of you than have you try to translate what I do."
IF NO / NOT NOW:
"Fair enough. Take 48 hours. I'll follow up Wednesday with one specific asset I think could work for you. Fair?"
Why this works: You are not pressuring; you are facilitating their decision. The "fair?" at the end of deferrals commits them to a specific next touchpoint.

Part 4: Objection Handling Scripts
"That's too expensive."
Response:
"I understand. Let me ask: If the audit showed you that [specific old tactic] could be modernized to generate [conservative estimate: 10 leads/month, $5K revenue, etc.], what would that be worth over the next year?
The audit is $[amount] one-time. The clarity it provides either validates a significant opportunity or prevents you from chasing the wrong tactics for another six months.
What would need to be true for this to feel like the right investment?"
If they still can't afford it:
"Fair enough. Here's what I can offer: The Shallow Dig. It's a 1-page summary based on 15 minutes of automated research. No charge. If what I find intrigues you, we can talk about the full audit when budget opens up. Interested?"

"I need to check with my partner."
Response:
"Absolutely. When do you think you could have that conversation? I'd rather schedule a brief 15-minute call with both of you than have you try to translate what I do. That way [Partner] can ask questions directly and we can get aligned on whether this makes sense."
If they won't schedule joint call:
"No problem. What questions do you anticipate [Partner] having? Let me give you clear answers you can take back."

"How do I know this will work?"
Response:
"The audit doesn't guarantee specific results—it guarantees clarity. You'll know exactly what worked, when, and why. You'll know which competitors are doing what. You'll have a specific modernization plan.
What happens after the audit—whether you implement the recommendations, hire us for Stage 2, or take the plan elsewhere—is up to you. But the diagnosis itself is certain. The historical record exists. We just need to excavate it.
Does that distinction make sense?"

"We've tried marketing consultants before."
Response:
"I hear that. Can I ask what went wrong? [Listen]
Here's the difference: Most consultants start with 'What should you do?' I start with 'What made you great?'
I'm not guessing about tactics or following a template. I'm researching your specific history, your market, your assets. The recommendations emerge from evidence, not opinion.
That said, if you're burned out on consultants, I get it. No hard feelings if this isn't the right time."

"Can you do it for less?"
Response:
"I don't discount the audit. The work is the work—20+ hours of research, analysis, documentation. If I cut corners, you get a worse product.
What I can do is adjust scope: Instead of 40+ channels, we focus on your top 10. Instead of 15–25 assets, we guarantee 8–10. Price becomes $[reduced amount].
Or we can explore payment terms: 25% to start, 25% at midpoint, 50% on delivery. Spreads the cash flow.
Which would be more helpful?"
