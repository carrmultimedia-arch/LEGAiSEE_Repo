SPECIFIC SEARCH SYNTAX BY CHANNEL
Wayback Machine (web.archive.org)
Basic:
plainCopy
https://web.archive.org/web/*/https://example.com
Deep patterns:
Campaign pages: */example.com/promo* or */example.com/offer*
Product launches: */example.com/products/[product-name]*
PDF archives: site:example.com filetype:pdf (in Google), then paste URLs into Wayback
Date filtering:
Add 2015 to URL for that year: https://web.archive.org/web/2015*/https://example.com
Compare snapshots:
Open 2 browser tabs: 2010 vs 2020 same URL
Screenshot side-by-side for "abandonment" evidence

YouTube (youtube.com)
Search operators:
plainCopy
"[company name]" commercial
"[company name]" "tv spot"
"[company name]" "founder" interview
"[company name]" training video
"[company name]" "banned" OR "vintage" OR "retro"
"[company name]" "[year]"  (e.g., "1998" "2005")
Filter tactics:
Filter → Upload date → Oldest first (find earliest content)
Filter → Type → Video (exclude Shorts for full commercials)
Filter → Duration → 4-20 minutes (training content, founder talks)
Channel investigation:
Find company's channel → Videos → Sort by oldest
Check "Playlists" for campaign groupings

Google News Archive (news.google.com/newspapers)
Search:
plainCopy
"[company name]" "advertising" OR "marketing" OR "campaign"
"[company name]" "announces" OR "launches" OR "introduces"
"[company name]" "[city]" "business profile"
"[founder name]" "[company name]" interview
Date restrict:
Add &tbs=cdr:1,cd_min:1990,cd_max:2005 to URL (1990-2005 range)

THE 4-QUERY AI PARALLEL STRUCTURE
Run these simultaneously in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity. Each has a different specialty.

QUERY 1: CHATGPT — "The Historian"
Role: Broad narrative, timeline construction, pattern recognition
plainCopy
You are a business historian researching [COMPANY NAME] ([WEBSITE]).

Construct a detailed timeline of their marketing evolution:

1. FOUNDING ERA: First marketing efforts, original positioning, founder story
2. GROWTH ERA: When and how they scaled marketing, channels used, key campaigns
3. PEAK ERA: Golden period — what worked best, metrics if available, industry recognition
4. DECLINE/TRANSITION: When tactics changed, what stopped, external pressures
5. CURRENT ERA: Present positioning, channels, messaging

For each era, identify:
- Specific campaigns or initiatives (names, dates, slogans)
- Channels used (be specific: which TV stations, which publications, which platforms)
- Visual identity elements (logo changes, color schemes, taglines)
- Budget indicators (hiring sprees, office expansions, sponsorship levels)

Output: Chronological narrative with specific evidence. Note gaps or uncertain dates.

QUERY 2: CLAUDE — "The Analyst"
Role: Psychological insights, competitive positioning, why things worked
plainCopy
You are a marketing strategist analyzing [COMPANY NAME]'s competitive positioning.

Focus on:

1. DIFFERENTIATION STRATEGY: What made them unique vs competitors? How did they communicate this?

2. CUSTOMER PSYCHOLOGY: Who did they target? What need did they meet? How did messaging evolve as markets changed?

3. COMPETITIVE RESPONSE: How did competitors react to their success? What gap opened when [COMPANY] changed tactics?

4. ABANDONMENT ANALYSIS: For each major tactic they stopped, analyze:
   - Official reason (if stated)
   - Likely real reason (market shift, leadership change, cost pressure)
   - What replaced it
   - Whether replacement worked as well

5. REVIVAL POTENTIAL: Which abandoned tactics could work today with modern execution? Why?

Output: Strategic analysis with psychological insights. Be specific about mechanisms, not just descriptions.

QUERY 3: GEMINI — "The Archivist"
Role: Specific artifact hunting, visual references, concrete examples
plainCopy
You are a digital archivist searching for specific marketing artifacts from [COMPANY NAME].

Find and describe:

1. VIDEO CONTENT:
   - Commercials (TV, web, social) — describe visuals, scripts, calls to action
   - Training videos, founder interviews, behind-the-scenes
   - Provide: Titles, approximate dates, where hosted (YouTube, Vimeo, company site)

2. PRINT/DIGITAL ADS:
   - Magazine/newspaper placements — describe visuals, headlines, offers
   - Direct mail pieces, catalogs, brochures
   - Website screenshots from specific years (describe key pages)

3. AUDIO/RADIO:
   - Jingles, radio spots, podcast appearances
   - Describe: Music style, voice tone, memorable phrases

4. PHYSICAL/EXPERIENTIAL:
   - Trade show booths, event sponsorships, branded merchandise
   - Store design, vehicle wraps, uniforms

5. DIGITAL FOOTPRINTS:
   - Social media campaigns, viral moments, hashtag campaigns
   - App launches, web tools, interactive content

For each artifact found, provide: Description, approximate date, channel/platform, why it mattered.

Output: Inventory of specific, tangible marketing assets with visual and contextual details.

QUERY 4: PERPLEXITY — "The Investigator"
Role: Real-time search, current status, verification, gaps
plainCopy
You are an investigative researcher verifying current status and finding gaps for [COMPANY NAME].

Execute real-time searches and report:

1. CURRENT MARKETING PRESENCE:
   - Active website sections, current campaigns, live promotions
   - Social media activity levels (posting frequency, engagement)
   - Active ad campaigns (Facebook Ad Library, Google Ads visibility)

2. RECENT CHANGES (Last 2-3 years):
   - Leadership changes affecting marketing
   - Rebrands, website redesigns, messaging pivots
   - New product launches or service discontinuations

3. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE NOW:
   - Who took their market position if they declined?
   - What are competitors doing that [COMPANY] is not?
   - Industry trends they may be missing

4. INFORMATION GAPS:
   - What years have sparse documentation?
   - What channels have no visible history?
   - What questions couldn't be answered?

5. VERIFICATION NEEDS:
   - Claims from other sources that need fact-checking
   - Contradictory information requiring resolution
   - Suspected but unconfirmed campaigns or dates

Output: Current snapshot with explicit gaps and verification priorities. Cite sources where possible.
